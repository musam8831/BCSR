# Scientific Evidence Brief — RES-02 v0.1 (scaffold)

Owner: AG-04 Research Analyst. Completed in T-M1-008. Every claim must cite a peer-reviewed source with a DOI. No uncited claims.

## 1. Research questions
- RQ1: Which passive smartphone signals are associated with stress, anxiety, low mood, poor sleep and loneliness? What effect sizes are reported?
- RQ2: What labelling methods (EMA, validated scales) are standard? Which instruments are licence-free and validated in Urdu?
- RQ3: Population vs personalised models: what improvement does personalisation bring?
- RQ4: What evidence exists for the effects of religious/spiritual coping, dhikr and prayer on stress (including Muslim populations)?
- RQ5: What are best practices for just-in-time adaptive interventions (JITAI): timing, receptivity, burden?

## 2. Starting literature (verify each and extract findings)
- **Digital phenotyping / passive sensing:** StudentLife (Wang et al., 2014, UbiComp); GLOBEM multi-year sensing dataset and benchmark (Xu et al., 2022/2023); systematic reviews on smartphone-sensing correlates of depression and stress (search terms: "smartphone passive sensing depression systematic review", "mobile sensing stress detection").
- **EMA methodology:** Shiffman, Stone & Hufford (2008), "Ecological Momentary Assessment", *Annual Review of Clinical Psychology*.
- **JITAI:** Nahum-Shani et al. (2018), "Just-in-Time Adaptive Interventions (JITAIs) in Mobile Health", *Annals of Behavioral Medicine*.
- **Religious coping:** Pargament's work on religious coping (Brief RCOPE); studies on dhikr/Qur'an recitation and anxiety (to be identified, with critical appraisal of quality).
- **Scales:** PSS-10 (Perceived Stress Scale) and its Urdu validation; single-item mood/energy EMA. PHQ/GAD screening only with mental-health advisor approval, and never presented as a diagnosis.
